Chinese Wedding Banquet Etiquette

New to Chinese wedding banquets? Here's what to expect:

  • Red envelopes: Skip the registry — a red envelope (lai see, 利是) is the classic move here. Unlike our Mérida wedding, we're not registered anywhere for the HK banquet, so cash in red is the way to go! Daphne will bring red envelopes for everyone, so no need to prep anything ahead of time.
  • Multi-course meal: Buckle up — expect a marathon, multi-course spread (often 8-10 courses!) over a few hours.
  • Toasting: Get ready to raise your glass — we'll be making the rounds for toasts throughout the night in Cantonese. Listen for "yum bui" (飲杯) or "gong bui" (乾杯) — that's your cue to cheers!
  • What to wear: Bright and festive is the vibe! Just steer clear of all-white or all-black (mourning colors) — and maybe leave the all-red outfit for Daphne.

The Tea Ceremony

Before the banquet, Daphne and Conner will take part in a traditional tea ceremony, kneeling before their parents and elders and serving them tea as a formal show of respect and gratitude. In return, elders offer blessings (and often a red envelope!) to the couple.
